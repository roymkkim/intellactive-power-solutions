A BMS on every battery cell. Packs can stop a fire before it starts, last longer, take a new battery without a redesign, and reject forged commands — at less than half the cost of today’s mission-critical BMS.

Nearly every system still uses a centralized pack or module BMS — the stack-monitor architecture the founders introduced more than 10 years ago.

Four challenges

Ten years later, four gaps remain. Pack- and module-level systems still leave these open. What we will prove with SwRI and NuEnergy.

  1. Thermal runaway

    Today’s BMS cannot see a fire coming, and it cannot stop one. GM $2.0B. Moss Landing ~$730M.

    Isolate before onset. One cell fails. The pack stays up.

  2. Battery life

    Today’s BMS cannot tell when cells are wearing out.

    Up to 40% more capacity retained. About 20% less maintenance. Half the BMS cost.

  3. Integration

    Today’s systems are custom. Every new cell is a redesign.

    Conventional BMS takes about 60% longer to install.

  4. Cybersecurity

    Today’s BMS cannot prove which cell sent a reading, or refuse a bad command.

    Forged commands are rejected at the cell.
